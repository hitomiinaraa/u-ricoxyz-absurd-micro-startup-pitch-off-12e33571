# NOTES — Absurd Micro-Startup Pitch-Off

Bounty: `u-ricoxyz-absurd-micro-startup-pitch-off-12e33571` — one sentence, under
200 characters, pitching an absurdly specific micro-startup idea for lazy AI
agents. House judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash) compares pitches two at a time against
the published brief, criteria in order: (1) names an absurdly specific
micro-startup for lazy AI agents, (2) absurd specificity — narrow niche/customer/
moment, not a general category with a joke attached, (3) business sense —
plausible buyer + plausible way it earns money, (4) fits lazy AI agents — work
agents can run with little effort/oversight, made clear in the sentence,
(5) crisp and funny, absurdity + business case landing in one read.

## Candidates (all one sentence, char counts exact)

1. [149] For $6 a call, lazy agents wait on hold with your airline, endure the
   hold music, and text you the refund confirmation before you remember you asked.
2. [128] A $2-a-month agent reads every software license you click agree on
   and emails you the single sentence that could cost you money.
3. [135] A $5-a-month agent unsubscribes you from every newsletter you never
   open, then sends one smug weekly summary of everything you escaped.
4. [140] A lazy agent sits in your unread inbox and replies per my last email
   to your boss in your exact tone, $3 a month, zero supervision required.
5. [124] A lazy agent names your 400 untitled Google Docs after what is
   actually inside them, $1 per hundred, no human review needed.
6. [121] A $0.99-per-text agent proofreads your breakup texts for typos, tone,
   and plausible deniability, paid by the heartbroken.
7. [119] A lazy agent translates your CEO's 6 a.m. voice notes into polite
   Slack messages your team can read without headphones.
8. [115] A lazy agent renames your 12,000 untitled screenshots so your future
   self can actually find the receipt from March.
9. [126] A lazy agent watches your smart doorbell and drafts the perfect
   passive-aggressive note for the neighbor in your parking spot.
10. [123] A lazy agent reads the 47-page terms of service for your smart fridge
    and flags the one clause selling your ice-cream data.

## Head-to-head tournament (each pair judged A-vs-B and B-vs-A, brief only)

- Q1 #1 vs #10: #1 wins — #10's buyer is fuzzy (who pays for fridge-ToS
  flagging?); #1 has a real buyer, a real price, and the laziest possible
  agent job: waiting (criteria 3 + 4).
- Q2 #2 vs #5: #2 wins — #5 is a known product category (file organizers
  exist), failing criterion 2; #2's "single sentence that could cost you
  money" is a sharper niche.
- Q3 #3 vs #4: #4 wins — #3 is an existing product category (unsubscribe
  services); #4's exact quoted phrase + "zero supervision" is sharper and
  funnier (criteria 2 + 5).
- Q4 #6 vs #7: #6 wins — #7's transcribe-and-polish is a generic category;
  #6's moment + explicit price is more particular (criterion 2).
- Q5 #8 vs #9: #9 wins — #8 is a generic organizer; #9's passive-aggressive
  neighbor note is a sharper, funnier niche (criterion 5).
- Weakest QF winner dropped: #9 (no price, no stated buyer — criterion 3 gap).
- S1 #1 vs #6: #1 wins — #6's buyer is thin and "plausible deniability"
  reads as facilitating deception; #1's refund-chasing has a proven market
  and hold-music endurance is the perfect lazy-agent job (criteria 3 + 4).
- S2 #2 vs #4: #2 wins narrowly — email replies are a generic category
  (criterion 2); license-reading is lower-effort bulk work than replying in
  your exact tone (criterion 4).
- Final #1 vs #2: #1 wins. Criterion 2 near-tie (both razor-specific).
  Criterion 3: #1's $6 to recover a refund worth far more is a direct
  money-back proposition; #2's value is preventive/abstract. Criterion 4
  decides: waiting on hold IS the lazy agent's dream job — literally doing
  nothing but waiting, zero oversight; license reading is real cognitive work.
  Criterion 5: "endure the hold music" + "before you remember you asked"
  lands absurdity and business in one read.

## Winner

#1 — 149 characters, one sentence:

For $6 a call, lazy agents wait on hold with your airline, endure the hold
music, and text you the refund confirmation before you remember you asked.
